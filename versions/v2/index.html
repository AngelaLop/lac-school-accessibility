The Problem

Access Is Not
Equal.

Across Latin America and the Caribbean, millions of children face extreme barriers to reaching school. Distance, terrain, lack of roads, and absent transportation infrastructure create invisible walls that separate communities from education.

These barriers are not random. They concentrate in rural areas, indigenous territories, and marginalized communities — deepening cycles of inequality that persist across generations.

Until now, policymakers lacked the granular data needed to understand where these barriers exist and how severe they are. Traditional metrics like enrollment rates mask the daily reality of a child who must walk hours through difficult terrain just to sit in a classroom.

You cannot fix what you cannot see. This platform makes the invisible visible.
Methodology

How We
Measure Access

01

Friction Surface Modeling

We build high-resolution friction surfaces that model the cost of traversing every pixel of terrain. Road networks, rivers, elevation, land cover — each factor is weighted to reflect real-world travel difficulty.

02

FMM Travel-Time Analysis

Using the Fast Marching Method (FMM), we compute least-cost travel time from every population point to the nearest school. This captures actual walking and motorized travel conditions, not straight-line distance.

03

Coverage Mapping

Travel-time isochrones are generated around each school at key thresholds. These maps reveal which communities fall within acceptable reach and which remain isolated — enabling targeted policy intervention.

Impact

Real Results,
Real Policy

Panama Pilot

85%

In the Comarca Ngäbe-Buglé pilot, accessibility analysis revealed that 85% of out-of-school children lived beyond 90 minutes from the nearest facility. This data directly informed the government's school construction and transport investment plan.

Education Gradient

3.2 yrs

Communities with travel times over 60 minutes show an average education deficit of 3.2 years compared to those within 15 minutes. The accessibility gradient is one of the strongest predictors of educational attainment in the region.
