The Challenge

Millions face impossible journeys

Across Latin America and the Caribbean, millions of children must travel extraordinary distances just to reach a classroom. In remote areas, schools are few, roads are scarce, and terrain is unforgiving.

Rural girls are disproportionately affected. When distances are long and travel is dangerous, families often keep girls home first. The result is a silent, systemic exclusion.

Until now, this data simply did not exist. No one had mapped where every school was, how far every child needed to travel, or which communities were most underserved. The LAC School Accessibility Platform changes that.

How It Works

Friction Mapping Model (FMM)

Three steps to measure how far every child is from school.

Step 1: Locate Every School

Harmonized school registries from 21 countries were compiled into a single, standardized database. Each school was georeferenced with precise latitude/longitude coordinates using a combination of official records, satellite imagery, and field validation.

532,650 schools located and georeferenced

Step 2: Map the Terrain

Satellite-derived friction surfaces model how difficult it is to traverse every square meter of land. These surfaces account for topography, land cover, road networks, rivers, and other geographic features that affect travel speed and accessibility.

1km² resolution of friction surface data

Step 3: Calculate Travel Time

The Friction Mapping Model (FMM) algorithm computes optimal travel paths from every population center to the nearest school. The result is a set of isochrones — contour lines showing how far someone can travel in a given time — for every school in the database.

Isochrones travel-time contour maps for each school
