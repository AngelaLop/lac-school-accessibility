The Challenge

When the road to school is the hardest lesson

Millions of children face impossible journeys

In the most remote corners of Latin America, children navigate rivers, mountains, and unpaved roads just to sit in a classroom. For families without transportation, a school that is 15 kilometers away might as well be on another continent.

The result is a silent crisis: children who drop out not because they lack motivation, but because geography stands in their way. Rural girls are disproportionately affected, often pulled from school first when the journey becomes too dangerous or too long.

Until now, the data simply did not exist

Governments knew the problem was real, but lacked the precise, comparable data to act on it. School locations were incomplete, travel times were estimated with guesswork, and the most vulnerable communities were the least visible on any map.

The LAC School Accessibility Platform changes this. By harmonizing school data across 21 countries and calculating realistic travel times, we give policymakers the evidence they need to build schools, plan bus routes, and invest where it matters most.

How It Works

From raw data to real understanding

The platform uses the Friction Map Methodology (FMM) to calculate how long it truly takes a child to reach school — not just the distance on a map, but the lived reality of terrain, roads, and rivers.

1

Locate every school

We harmonized official school registries from 21 countries, cleaning and georeferencing 532,650 schools with precise coordinates. Public and private, urban and rural — every school counts.

2

Map the terrain

Using satellite data, we built friction surfaces that capture the real cost of travel: paved roads, dirt paths, rivers, mountains, and forests. Each pixel represents how hard it is to cross that piece of land.

3

Calculate travel time

The FMM algorithm finds the fastest realistic path from every point on the map to the nearest school, producing travel-time isochrones that reveal exactly which communities are underserved.

Panama: A story of proximity and progress

In Panama, our analysis found that 98.1% of the school-age population lives within 30 minutes of a school by motorized transport. That is the good news. The harder truth is what happens to the remaining 1.9% — children in indigenous comarcas and remote provinces where the nearest school can be hours away.

The platform pinpoints exactly where these gaps exist, enabling targeted investment in the communities that need it most.

The education gradient

As travel time increases, enrollment drops. The pattern is consistent across every country we studied.

Primary School

Highest enrollment rates. Most communities have a primary school nearby. But even here, remote areas see 15–20% lower attendance than urban centers.

Secondary School

Fewer schools, longer distances. Rural students are 3x more likely to drop out at this level. For girls, the risk is even higher when journeys exceed 30 minutes.

Upper Secondary

The steepest drop-off. In the most remote municipalities, upper secondary completion can fall below 20%. Distance becomes the decisive barrier.
