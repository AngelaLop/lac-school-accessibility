The Challenges

Geography as Gatekeeper

Across Latin America, the landscape itself decides who gets an education.

Winding, Unpaved Roads

In rural communities, roads may be unpaved, washed out by rain, or simply absent. What appears as a short distance on a map becomes hours of difficult travel on foot.

40% of rural roads in LAC are unpaved or seasonal

Mountain Barriers

The Andes stretch across seven countries, creating extreme elevation changes that multiply travel time. A school may be just 5 km away but separated by a 1,000-meter climb.

6,900 km of Andean mountain range crossing LAC

River Crossings

In the Amazon basin and Caribbean lowlands, rivers are both highways and barriers. Children must canoe or wait for boats, adding unpredictable time to every journey.

6.9M km² Amazon basin area where rivers define daily life

Methodology

How It Works

A three-step framework using the Fast Marching Method on terrain-based friction surfaces.

Map All Schools

We geolocate every K-12 school across 21 LAC countries by combining national education registries, satellite imagery, and field verification. The result is a georeferenced database of 532,650 schools with 96% coordinate accuracy. Public schools account for 447,000 of these, with the remaining 86,000 being private institutions. Each school record includes location, education level, and administrative metadata.

447K Public schools
86K Private schools
96% Georeferenced

Build Friction Surface

We construct a terrain-based friction surface that models how difficult it is to move through every point in the landscape. This surface integrates road networks (paved and unpaved), rivers and waterways, elevation and slope data, land cover classifications, and built-up area boundaries. Each cell receives a friction value representing the time cost to cross it, whether by walking, canoeing, or motorized transport.

5 Data layers fused
~1 km Grid resolution
21 Country surfaces

Propagate Travel-Time Wavefront

Using the Fast Marching Method (FMM), we propagate a travel-time wavefront outward from every school simultaneously. The wavefront expands through the friction surface, following the path of least resistance. The result: for any point on the map, we know the minimum travel time to the nearest school — for both walking and motorized scenarios. This lets us identify underserved communities and quantify access gaps at national and regional scale.
