The Challenge

Millions of children across Latin America and the Caribbean face impossible journeys to reach school each day. In remote and rural areas, the nearest classroom can be hours away by foot, boat, or unpaved road.

Rural girls are disproportionately affected. When the journey to school is dangerous or long, families often keep daughters home first. The data to understand these patterns simply did not exist — until now.

The LAC School Accessibility Platform harmonizes school location data across 21 countries, creating the first unified geospatial picture of educational access in the region.

step_3 run_fmm() output: travel_time_isochrones

How It Works

The Friction Map Model (FMM) transforms satellite imagery and road networks into a continuous travel-time surface.

01

Locate Every School

Harmonized national registries produce 532,650 georeferenced school locations across 21 countries.

02

Map the Terrain

Satellite-derived friction surfaces model roads, rivers, mountains, and land cover. Each pixel gets a traversal cost.

03

Calculate Travel Time

The FMM algorithm computes least-cost paths, generating travel-time isochrones from every populated cell to the nearest school.
