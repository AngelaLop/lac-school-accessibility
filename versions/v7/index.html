02.

The Problem

Across Latin America and the Caribbean, millions of children face journeys of an hour or more just to reach a classroom. Distance is the invisible filter — it determines who learns and who doesn't. Without precise travel-time data, governments allocate resources blind.

4 in 10 Rural students travel 60+ min each way
80% Dropout rate linked to distance
0 Countries had full travel-time maps before this project
03.

The Method

The platform uses the Fast Marching Method (FMM) — an algorithm from computational physics that models wavefront propagation across friction surfaces. Every pixel of terrain is assigned a traversal cost based on land cover, slope, road networks, and waterways. The result: a precise, continuous travel-time surface from any point to its nearest school.

  • Step A Ingest school coordinates from national registries — 532,650 points across 21 countries.
  • Step B Build a friction surface from satellite-derived land cover, terrain slope, road class, and hydrological barriers.
  • Step C Run FMM to compute travel time from every 100 m cell to the nearest school, producing a continuous isochrone map.
  • Step D Overlay population grids to estimate the number of children within each travel-time band (15, 30, 60, 120 min).
04.

In Panama, the pilot deployment identified underserved communities and directly informed infrastructure investment by the Ministry of Education.
